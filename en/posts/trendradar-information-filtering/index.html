Overwhelmed by Information? TrendRadar Finds Useful Signals in the Noise

Eva·2026.04.23·13 min read

 

Does this sound familiar?

You open your phone every morning: 50 trending topics on Weibo, 30 recommended Zhihu articles, a pile of Bilibili notifications, and countless articles forwarded in group chats. After an hour of scrolling, your mind is a blur—you feel you have seen everything and absorbed nothing.

Worse still, the information that actually matters to you—industry developments, news about companies you follow, and relevant technology trends—is buried under celebrity gossip and trending chatter before you can get to it.

This is not your fault. It follows from the business logic of today's information platforms.Their goal is to keep you there longer, not to help you obtain valuable information.

What if you had a personal information assistant?

Imagine this:

Every morning at 8, a message arrives through Feishu, email, or a phone notification. It has already selected what matters to you from a dozen platforms such as Zhihu, Weibo, 36Kr, and Bilibili, with AI analysis attached: “Technology sentiment is optimistic today, with two unusual signals worth watching…”

You never need to open an app, filter things manually, or follow an algorithm's lead.

That is what TrendRadar does.

It is a fully open-source, self-hostable intelligent information radar with a simple core idea:You define what matters; it finds the signal in the noise.

Who is it for?

  • 📈 Investors: Monitor market sentiment, industry developments, and relevant company news in real time
  • ✍️ Content creators: Follow trending topics, find ideas, and catch emerging opportunities for attention
  • 📣 PR and brand professionals: Monitor brand mentions and competitors, and respond promptly to public discussion
  • 🔬 Technology researchers: Follow open-source ecosystems and technology trends while filtering irrelevant information
  • 😩 Everyday users: Avoid the daily information barrage and see only what matters to them

How does it differ from an ordinary news app?


Ordinary news app / RSS readerTrendRadar
Who decides what you see?Platform algorithmsYour own rules + AI
Trend analysis?NoYes, LLM-powered multidimensional analysis
Where is the data?Platform serversYour own machine or cloud storage
Where can it send notifications?In-app notifications9+ channels including Feishu, DingTalk, Telegram, and email
Programmable extensions?Generally noSupports MCP and AI-toolchain integration

Put simply, ordinary information tools offer passive reception, while TrendRadar offers active retrieval.

An example

Suppose you are an investor following the AI industry. You write a sentence in ai_interests.txt:

我关注 AI 创业公司的融资动态、大模型的新产品发布，以及 AI 监管政策的变化

TrendRadar passes that natural-language description to an LLM, which finds items matching your intent among all the trending content collected that day—without manually maintaining keyword lists.

After filtering, it also generates analysis:

Key trends today: Discussion of AI agents is up 40%, and several outlets are covering a new large-model release...

Unusual signals: Discussion of an AI company's share price has spiked over the past two hours, with negative sentiment...

Outlook and suggestions: Watch for policy-related news tomorrow...

It then sends this to your Feishu account or inbox.The whole process is automatic. You only need to read the report.

With that intuition in place, let's look at the technology behind TrendRadar.

Introduction: A structural dilemma in the information age

The internet has brought humanity into an era of information overload. According to IDC data, more than 2.5 quintillion bytes are generated globally every day, most of which are noise to any individual. This is particularly acute for investment analysts, creators, public-opinion monitors, and technology researchers, who must extract high-signal information from vast cross-platform feeds within limited time.

Traditional solutions—RSS readers, aggregators, and manual subscriptions—remain at the aggregation stage, without solving the central problems of filtering and analysis. The deeper dilemma is that platform recommendation algorithms optimize for time spent, rather than users' actual information needs. This is fundamentally a misalignment of incentives.

TrendRadar is an open-source intelligent information radar that turns passive consumption into active retrieval through an end-to-end “aggregate → filter → analyze → deliver” pipeline. This article examines its architecture, core mechanisms, and engineering design in depth.

System positioning and design boundaries

Core problem domain

TrendRadar primarily addresses three problems:

ProblemLimitations of traditional solutionsTrendRadar's approach
Information overloadInefficient manual selection and dependence on recommendationsKeyword engine + AI semantic filtering to define interests proactively
Platform fragmentationSwitching between platforms without a unified viewUnified acquisition layer with one entry point for 11+ platforms
Missing analysisRaw information without trend insightsLLM-driven analysis across five dimensions

Target users

The system is optimized for these scenarios:

  • Quantitative investors: Need low-latency market-sentiment signals and timely data
  • PR and public-opinion monitoring: Need near-real-time tracking of brand mentions and competitor activity
  • Content creators: Follow hot topics and need an aggregated cross-platform popularity view
  • Technology researchers: Follow open-source ecosystems and technology trends, requiring high-quality information with noise removed

1. Data Acquisition Layer

This layer is the system's data entry point, retrieving raw data from heterogeneous sources and standardizing it.

NewsNow API client wraps 11+ major platforms, including Zhihu, Weibo, Bilibili, Douyin, Baidu trending searches, and 36Kr. Its unified interface hides API differences. Key design points include:

  • Automatic rate-limit handling and retries with exponential backoff
  • URL normalization: removing UTM tracking parameters, standardizing schemes, and normalizing paths
  • Timezone conversion to the user's configured local timezone

RSS/Atom fetcher Built using the feedparser library, it supports custom feeds. Field mappings convert different formats, including RSS 2.0 and Atom 1.0, into the system's unified internal NewsItem data class.

2. Scheduling & Control Layer

The scheduling layer is the system's control plane, coordinating execution timing throughout the workflow.

Scheduler (located in trendradar/core/scheduler.py) reads config/timeline.yaml and converts user-defined schedules into the ResolvedSchedule data class:

@dataclass
class ResolvedSchedule:
    collect: bool    # 当前时间点是否采集数据
    analyze: bool    # 是否触发 AI 分析
    push: bool       # 是否推送通知
    report_mode: str # daily / current / incremental

Execution deduplication uses the period_executions database table to track daily execution records. The once_per_day flag prevents duplicate notifications even if the service restarts within the same time window, which is critical to production stability.

3. Core Processing Engine

The core engine is the system's most complex part, implementing a sequential, multistage data-processing pipeline:

原始数据
   ↓ [1] URL 规范化
   ↓ [2] 全局过滤器（[GLOBAL_FILTER] 排除规则）
   ↓ [3] 关键词匹配（正则 / 纯文本 / AI 语义兴趣）
   ↓ [4] 必需词检查（+前缀词 AND 逻辑）
   ↓ [5] 分组过滤（!前缀词触发组内互斥排除）
   ↓ [6] 权重计算
   ↓ [7] 新内容检测（对比历史数据，标记 🆕）
   ↓ [8] AI 处理（翻译 / 分析 / 摘要）
   ↓ [9] 输出生成（HTML + 多渠道通知）

The weighting model uses a weighted linear combination:

综合权重 = 排名得分 × 0.6 + 频次得分 × 0.3 + 平台热度 × 0.1

The ranking score comes from the original platform ranking. Frequency measures how often the same topic appears across platforms—cross-platform resonance strongly indicates a valuable signal. The platform popularity coefficient is preset based on audience size.

Keyword-filter syntax is thoughtfully designed and supports three filtering modes:

SyntaxMeaningExample
wordOrdinary keyword (OR logic)AI
+wordRequired term (AND logic)+股票
!wordWithin-group exclusion!明星
[GROUP]Group definition[科技]

This syntax lets users define complex filtering rules without writing code, lowering the barrier to use.

AppContext (trendradar/context.py) acts as a dependency-injection container, managing all core services' lifecycles and avoiding global singletons and tight coupling.

4. Storage Layer

The storage layer uses the Strategy Pattern to select an appropriate backend automatically for the deployment environment:

  • LocalStorageBackend: Writes to local SQLite with separate daily databases (output/news/YYYY-MM-DD.db), suitable for a single-machine deployment
  • RemoteStorageBackend: Supports Cloudflare R2, AWS S3, Alibaba Cloud OSS, and Tencent Cloud COS. Its “download existing DB → merge incremental data → upload to cloud” workflow provides persistence for stateless containers

Daily SQLite partitioning balances query performance—historical data is excluded from scans—with management convenience: deleting files removes old data.

5. AI Integration Layer

This layer uses LiteLLM to provide unified access to 100+ LLM providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and local Ollama. Users can switch the underlying model through configuration without vendor lock-in.

Structured prompts drive five built-in analytical dimensions:

  1. 1. Core trends: Identify dominant narratives and topic clusters in the current feed
  2. 2. Sentiment and controversy: Analyze public sentiment and identify highly contentious topics
  3. 3. Unusual signals: Detect breaking events, black-swan signals, and statistical anomalies
  4. 4. RSS insights: Produce in-depth summaries of feed data
  5. 5. Outlook and strategy: Offer forward-looking suggestions based on current trends

max_news_for_analysis (150 items by default) limits how much data goes into the LLM at once, balancing analytical quality against token cost.

Architectural design principles

TrendRadar demonstrates several engineering principles worth learning from:

1. Separation of Concerns (SoC): Each of the six layers has a clear responsibility. Data acquisition excludes business logic, the AI layer ignores data origins, and distribution is independent of data content.

2. Dependency Injection (DI): AppContext serves as an IoC container managing service instances, avoiding scattered global state and hard-coded dependencies while improving testability.

3. Strategy Pattern: Storage backends, filtering modes, and notification channels use strategies selected by runtime configuration. New extensions do not require changing core logic.

4. Pipeline Pattern: Data processing is divided into ordered, composable stages, each feeding its output into the next, simplifying debugging and unit testing.

5. Graceful Degradation: Failed AI analysis falls back to keyword filtering; unavailable remote storage falls back to local SQLite; one failed platform fetch does not disrupt the overall workflow.

6. Idempotent design: Execution deduplication makes operations idempotent within a time window, allowing safe restarts and retries as required for production stability.

7. Configuration-Driven: Declarative configuration defines core behavior instead of imperative code, lowering the barrier to customization and secondary development.

Conclusion

TrendRadar illustrates a shift from information-consumption tools toward information-analysis systems. It integrates retrieval previously scattered across apps into a configurable, extensible, AI-enhanced automated pipeline.

From an engineering perspective, its six-layer architecture, strategy-based storage, dependency-injected service management, and configuration-driven behavior reflect mature practices. For individuals and teams with intensive information needs, it provides highly customizable open-source infrastructure for building their own retrieval workflows.

Information asymmetry is, at heart, a moat around decision quality. TrendRadar aims to make that advantage accessible to more people, which may be its most important contribution.
